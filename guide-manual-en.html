02 — Images & nodes

Drop an image to create a node.

  1. Drag an image onto empty canvas space.

    A thumbnail node appears immediately. PNG, JPEG and WebP images up to 2MB each are supported.

  2. Add a source and interpretation in Node interpretation.

    Enter the image source (up to 500 characters) and interpretation (up to 200 characters), then save.

  3. Click the node again to edit.

    If a source or interpretation is already saved, the button reads Update.

Dropping a new image onto an existing node preserves the original image and creates a new node nearby with a directed link from the original. Fill in the new node first; click the link later to interpret it.

Editing & deletion

Local mode checks the browser identity; shared mode checks the account ID for node editing and deletion. Older nodes with unknown creators are read-only. Changing a display name does not change ownership.

Select an item and use Delete in the toolbar. Deleting a node also removes its links, while other nodes remain. Local deletions can be undone. Shared deletions require confirmation and cannot be undone. Delete/Backspace shortcuts apply only when the canvas has focus, not while the writing panel is open.

03 — Links & interpretations

Keep multiple interpretations on one link.

  1. Use Create link to connect existing nodes.

    Click the source and target nodes in order. Links are directed.

  2. Choose a relationship family and specific type.

    Define the relationship in the editor beside the link. Add several interpretations, then save them together.

  3. Comment on saved interpretations.

    Comments appear in creation order. Agree or veto each comment, then change or remove your reaction if needed.

Deleting a comment also removes its reactions, but not the interpretation or node. To delete the link itself, select it on the canvas and use Delete in the toolbar.

A relationship named Cause is still a proposed interpretation. The ontology does not establish causality as fact.

Understanding and choosing relationships

First, write one sentence explaining which part of A relates to which part of B, and how. Choose the relationship that fits that sentence, then describe its scope and your reasoning in the definition.

1. Separate link direction from relationship meaning

In A → B, A is the source node and B is the target. The arrow alone does not imply causation, chronological order or approval. The examples below illustrate ways to read a relationship; its actual meaning must be read together with the participant’s definition.

The Cause and Effect examples both use the direction “A leads to B.” Cause emphasizes A’s contribution; Effect emphasizes the change in B. Selecting Effect does not reverse the arrow. If you mean “A is a result of B,” state this explicitly in the definition, or reverse the link to B → A and review the definition.

2. Logical relationships: explaining and comparing

Eight logical relationships
RelationshipWhen to choose itA → B example and definition
CauseWhen to choose itChoose this when interpreting A as causing or contributing to B.A → B example and definitionA introduces automation → B has less repetitive work: “Automation is interpreted as contributing to the reduction in repetitive work.”
EffectWhen to choose itChoose this to emphasize the change or outcome in B attributed to A. In this example, A is the cause and B is the effect.A → B example and definitionA introduces automation → B has greater demand for retraining: “Demand for retraining is interpreted as an outcome of introducing automation.”
PremiseWhen to choose itChoose this when A is an assumption or condition needed for the argument in B.A → B example and definitionA ensures data quality → B uses AI analysis: “The analysis is used on the condition that the data is accurate.”
EvidenceWhen to choose itChoose this when data, observations or examples in A support a claim in B. Providing evidence differs from claiming a cause.A → B example and definitionA user survey → B argues for training: “Difficulties reported in the survey are presented as evidence for the need for training.”
ComplementWhen to choose itChoose this when A and B fill gaps in each other and are useful together.A → B example and definitionA statistics → B interviews: “Statistics show the scale of change, while interviews describe how people experience it.”
ContrastWhen to choose itChoose this to highlight differences between A and B using the same comparison criteria. They need not oppose or conflict with each other.A → B example and definitionA rapid adoption → B phased adoption: “Compare the pace of adoption and approaches to risk management for the same goal.”
SimilarityWhen to choose itChoose this to describe a feature or structure shared by A and B. Similarity does not establish the same causes or conclusions.A → B example and definitionCase A → case B: “Both cases are similar in that users received insufficient training.”
Part and wholeWhen to choose itChoose this when one item is a component of another. State which is the part and which is the whole.A → B example and definitionA privacy policy → B AI governance: “A is one of the policies that make up B.”

A premise is a condition for an argument; evidence is material that supports a claim. Contrast compares differences; opposition is a stance against a claim or proposal.

3. Political and stance relationships: positions and actions

This family is not limited to political parties or political news. Use it to describe how people, groups or positions relate to each other, or how they act together.

Six political and stance relationships
RelationshipWhen to choose itA → B example and definition
SupportWhen to choose itChoose this when a person, group or position in A backs a proposal or action in B.A → B example and definitionA user group → B proposal to expand training: “A supports putting B’s proposal into practice.”
OppositionWhen to choose itChoose this when A objects to or rejects a claim or proposal in B.A → B example and definitionGroup A → B full rollout proposal: “A opposes B’s rollout proposal because of its cost.”
AgreeWhen to choose itChoose this when A and B share a position on a particular judgment or claim. Agreement does not necessarily imply practical support.A → B example and definitionA expert opinion → B research conclusion: “A agrees with B’s assessment of the risk.”
ConflictWhen to choose itChoose this when the goals, interests or demands represented by A and B clash.A → B example and definitionA cost-cutting goal → B demand for job security: “The two demands conflict over staffing decisions.”
CooperationWhen to choose itChoose this when A and B combine their roles or resources to pursue a shared goal.A → B example and definitionA university → B company: “The university provides teaching and the company provides placements to train people together.”
AlternativeWhen to choose itChoose this when presenting A as an option to consider instead of B. State what it would replace.A → B example and definitionA phased pilot → B immediate full rollout: “A is proposed as an alternative to reduce the cost and risk of B.”

4. Write a specific relationship definition

A useful template is: “The [content] in A has a [relationship] to the [content] in B, because [material or reasoning], within [scope or uncertainty].” Explain how you interpret the connection rather than simply repeating the relationship name.

Example: “Responses in A’s survey reporting insufficient training are treated as evidence for B’s proposal to expand training. The survey covers one organization, so the interpretation is not generalized to the whole industry.”

Even for comparisons such as Similarity, Contrast and Complement, the system does not automatically create a reverse link. State your comparison criteria in the definition. Add a reverse link and interpretation only when you need to record a separate meaning in the other direction.

5. Distinguish relationships, comments and reactions

Support or Agree as a relationship type describes the connection between A and B. Support, Do not support and Undecided on a comment describe a participant’s stance toward that interpretation. Agree or Veto on a comment is a reaction to that comment. Reaction counts do not establish whether an interpretation is true or adopted.

If participants read the same link differently, use Add another interpretation to keep each definition. The AI receives the saved relationship names, definitions, authors and comments together, so clear definitions help it follow the intended meaning.

New canvas saves the current canvas and opens an empty one. Only canvases containing nodes become chips. Repeatedly creating an empty canvas adds no chips. Labels use the first saved timestamp, YYYY-MM-DD HH:mm:ss, and remain stable. Click a chip to return. Deleting a personal canvas permanently removes its local contents after confirmation; removing a shared chip only removes the list entry. Lists are stored per account in this browser. Back up each canvas to move devices.

04 — AI writing

Write from canvas context

The top button opens a wide drawer from the right and shifts the canvas left. Closing and reopening preserves input. Close this panel before using backup/restore or creating links.

1. API settings2. Writing guide3. Writing result
  1. API settings

    Start with API settings. Choose Gemini, OpenAI, Claude or a local OpenAI-compatible API. Enter the key and model, then save. Load models or enter an ID manually. Changing connection details cancels an in-flight model request; load the list again. Local APIs also require a server URL.

    Save settings again after changing the provider, key or model. API settings remain only in this page’s memory; enter them again after closing the browser or tab, reloading or navigating away. They are excluded from workspace backups. Saving settings does not generate text.

  2. Writing guide

    The whole canvas is included, regardless of selection: nodes, link directions, interpretations and comments, including disconnected nodes. Choose genre, purpose, audience, format, length, perspective and principles, then save the guide.

    For nonfiction, choose an explanatory article, essay, report or opinion column. For literature, choose a story or essay. Switching genres remembers your selection for each genre. A different format in an older guide is marked as previously saved and is not changed automatically.

    Save again after changing materials or settings. AI receives the saved guide text; original images are not sent.

  3. Writing result

    Use Ask AI to write or Generate text explicitly. New generation is blocked until API setup is complete, but saved/shared results remain readable. Opening a tab does not call AI. Requesting text from the guide moves keyboard focus to the result tab.

    Cancel while generating. Failed or cancelled requests retain the previous result. Copy the result or download it as TXT.

Usage is billed to your provider account. If direct browser access is blocked, check provider or local-server CORS settings. Review generated text and sources before use.

Narrative order & generation evidence

Let AI choose the structure or order nodes manually. Move earlier/later changes presentation order only, not link direction, chronology or causality. Save the guide after changing it.

AI receives the same vocabulary semantics shown in the ontology definitions plus the saved network of nodes, links, interpretations, comments and reactions. Targets and authors are distinguished; uninterpreted links and disconnected materials remain. Sending these rules does not semantically validate the output.

Knowledge structure includes saved guides, material snapshots, actual AI input, generating model and results. Resaving a guide preserves previous result prompts. If an older structured snapshot is missing, only a reference is shown. Unrecorded requesters and paragraph-level evidence are not inferred. These links do not guarantee factual accuracy.

05 — Ontology structure

Explore the knowledge you have built.

Open Ontology structure to see the actual JSON-LD graph. Select an item to inspect details, identifiers and applicable constraints.

Ontology opens in a right drawer like writing: 75vw on desktop, full width at 800px or below. Only the viewport shifts. Choose Knowledge structure, Canvas connections or Ontology definitions. Writing and ontology open one at a time; writing inputs are retained. Use Close to return.

Knowledge structure

Explore images, links, interpretations, relationship types, comments, reactions and authors. View the whole graph or one link.

Canvas connections

Inspect image-to-image direction and interpretation. Dashed links await interpretation. Click a link label to explore its knowledge structure.

Ontology definitions

Inspect RDF/OWL classes and properties, SHACL constraints, class counts and property usage.

Physics adjusts spacing automatically. Pause/resume layout, zoom, pan and drag items. Multiple and reverse relations use separate curves. The item selector provides keyboard access. Graph layout does not alter the canvas. JSON-LD export is enabled only for administrators and requires online verification. Backup/restore is separate.

Displaying SHACL constraints is not the same as executing validation. This screen does not show automated reasoning results or replay change history.

07 — Collaboration & online storage

Work together on the same canvas.

  1. Open Account & workspace and sign in under Sign in & collaborate.

    Register by email and verify the account. Signing in alone does not share your work.

  2. Open Share your current canvas.

    Name and create a shared copy. You own the copied items; previous author labels remain. Existing reactions are marked as unverified history. The original local workspace remains.

  3. Share an invitation code.

    Create a code and send it directly to chosen participants. They sign in and join with the code. It expires after 7 days and can be revoked or replaced by the owner.

  4. Drop images and save interpretations and comments.

    Item-level changes save automatically to the database and appear for other participants. Check server save/pending/error states in the footer. Incoming changes wait while you are editing.

Shared dataPrivate information
Images, node positions, sources, interpretations, directed links, definitions, comments, reactions, saved guides and writing resultsAPI provider, model, key, server URL, authentication tokens, current selection and zoom

A result's model name records provenance. Others cannot use your API configuration. Shared results remain readable without API setup, but new generation requires your own settings.

Permissions & conflicts

Node interpretation editing/deletion, link deletion and comment deletion require authorship. Participants can position nodes and add images, links, interpretations and comments. Deleting a node also deletes its links and their contents. Shared deletions require confirmation and cannot be undone.

Concurrent edits to the same item are not silently overwritten. Your changes are preserved with a notice. Back up your edits and load the latest version to compare. Edits to different items can merge. Keystrokes and mouse cursors are not broadcast live.

Unsent edits are kept separately in this browser after connection failure. Retry saving or reopen the same canvas in the same tab/account to recover. Download a backup before fully closing a tab with pending changes. Returning to local work or signing out restores the previous local canvas.

Personal online copies

Expand Personal online copies to save/load snapshots. Users can access their own copies. This is not automatic sync; each save creates a new copy. The most recent 50 appear. Ordinary users have no delete button; administrators can hide/delete copies in Content.

Canvas list & workspace

On desktop, My canvases sits on the left. A cool-white background from the shared color tokens extends below the list, with a thin right-hand border separating it from the canvas. At widths of 800px or less, the list becomes a horizontal scrolling row above the canvas. Opening a saved canvas fits its nodes to the available workspace.

Administrator tools

Administrators see Users and Content after Backup & restore. Inspect online content by user and hide/delete shared canvases, shared results and personal copies. Hiding is reversible; deletion is permanent. Local-only and downloaded copies are outside this management scope.

Design-system access & storage status

Donate

Open Donate before New canvas for donation information. Only the last three account digits are shown, with no copy function. Donate with Toss opens a transfer screen; desktop shows a QR code. Only Close dismisses the modal. Donations are optional.

The design-system link requires verified administrator access for worflogy@gmail.com. It is disabled for guests and other users. The user guide is public.

Local means stored in this browser. Signing in does not upload work. Save personal online copies explicitly; shared canvases sync changes to the server. Check the footer status.

Administrators can hide, restore or delete online content, but cannot delete or suspend accounts through this interface. Image files may remain after content deletion to preserve reused images.

Author display

The default author is the login email, including on new canvases. Custom names and existing records are retained. Only your own nodes, interpretations and comments display (me); other participants see the stored name. Ownership IDs determine identity and editing rights, not names. Unverified older records do not receive (me).

Interface language

Use the Korean/English buttons at the top to switch the interface. Unsaved input and canvas data are retained. User contributions and existing AI results are not automatically translated. New AI requests from the English interface ask for English output.

Manage saved interpretations

Manage saved interpretations using Edit interpretation and Delete interpretation below the relationship name. Only the author may make changes. Editing retains existing comments, agreements and vetoes; deleting also removes that interpretation’s comments and reactions. The link and other interpretations remain.

Reverse link direction

Selecting a link shows its source and target images. The link author can reverse the arrow with Reverse direction. Interpretations and comments remain unchanged and should be checked against the new direction. Shared canvases require the database update for direction changes.

Confirmations and notifications

For deletions and important changes, an in-app dialog explains the target and impact. Cancel receives initial focus, and action buttons use explicit labels such as Delete or Switch. Close and Cancel do not perform the action. Clicking outside or pressing Escape does not dismiss the dialog. Completion notices use toasts, and input errors appear near the affected field.

Review source materials

Writing materials are summarized at the top with counts of images, links, interpretations and comments. Expand images to review interpretations and sources, or links to review their source, target, interpretations and comments. All canvas materials are included regardless of which lists are expanded. Original image files are not sent to the AI.

AI instructions

The AI guide summarizes the saved purpose, audience, format, perspective, narrative order and writing principles. Expand View full AI instructions to inspect the actual document. Copy, download and AI requests always use the full instructions, including the ontology and canvas materials.

Interpretation panels

Node interpretation presents the image and title, source, interpretation field and save action in order. Relationship interpretation shows the direction and interpretation list, with the selected definition, author, edit and delete actions grouped in one card. Expand Leave a comment to write an opinion; existing comments are shown oldest first with agree and veto actions. Existing author permissions still apply.

Reading writing results

Writing results display headings, paragraphs, emphasis and lists. Long identifiers become numbered references, with saved node and relationship interpretations listed below the text. Identifiers without matching saved materials are marked as unresolved. Reference numbers do not indicate fact-checking.

Copy and download provide readable text and references. View original AI response shows the exact response, including identifiers and Markdown. Stored responses and AI input materials remain unchanged.

Resume your last canvas

In the same browser, reloading or returning from the user guide or design system reopens the last canvas used by your signed-in account. Shared canvases reopen after server access is checked. If a shared canvas cannot be opened, your personal canvas is shown with a notice. Choosing Return to local work makes that personal canvas your next starting point. The last selection is not synced across devices.

Sign in with Google

Choose Continue with Google, select your Google account, and return to the canvas. To continue your existing work, use the Google account with the email you used to sign up. If Google setup is not complete, you can still sign in with email. Google sign-in is supported on the published site or localhost, not when opening the HTML file directly.

Delete account

In Account & workspace management, open Delete account on the Sign in & collaborate tab. Review what will be deleted, enter your login email and confirm permanent deletion. Back up any work you need first. Local work and downloaded files remain.

AI API settings lifetime

The AI provider, model, API key and server address remain only in the current page’s memory. Enter them again after closing the browser or tab, reloading, or navigating away. Previously persisted API settings are also removed. Your canvas, saved writing guide and generated text are retained.
